Treating man as a "mere means"

Published: August 2026

In Immanuel Kant's Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals, the Formula of Humanity dictates: "Act in such a way that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in the person of any other, never merely as a means to an end, but always at the same time as an end."

1. Means vs. "Mere" Means

There is a crucial distinction between using someone as a means versus a mere means. When you order tea from a Dudhcha vendor, you treat them as a means to get tea—but if you pay them fairly, respect their agency, and recognize their autonomy, you treat them as an end simultaneously.

Treating someone as a mere means occurs when you involve them in a scheme to which they could not, in principle, consent—such as deception, coercion, or exploitation.
